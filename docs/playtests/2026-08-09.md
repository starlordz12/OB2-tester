# Orbit or Bust 2 beta — visible-build playtest report

## Executive result

The external tester played the real, visible Godot executable `OrbitOrBust2.exe`. The tested file's SHA-256 is `94CA7490BB67F23E34BEB87B8C73E7D85D6C767A259BC2A90C57C39F0BB0EC42`.

The fastest verified live orbit was achieved by the **SRB preset / Strap-On Orbiter** in **215.7 mission-seconds**. The controller required two consecutive rendered periapsis readings above 71 km and then cut thrust. Its result record was:

- Altitude: 85.98 km
- Apoapsis: 134.13 km
- Periapsis: 72.02 km
- Remaining fuel: 3.47 t
- Profile: turn at 350 m, horizontal by 30 km, initial target Ap 82 km, maximum atmospheric angle of attack 0.28 rad

The evidence screenshot taken moments later visibly reads T+03:36.0, Ap 137.19 km, Pe 72.77 km, thrust 0 kN, and the game's own “ORBIT! Periapsis is above the atmosphere” message. The map capture shows a closed trajectory outside the atmospheric band.

## What the game does well

- **Orbit success is finally unambiguous.** Ap, Pe, the 70 km atmosphere reference, the explicit ORBIT message, and the map all agree. This is far better than inferring success from altitude alone.
- **The flight HUD exposes the right information.** Stage and total fuel, mass, thrust, dynamic pressure, vertical speed, SAS, Ap/Pe, and time to apoapsis support real piloting decisions.
- **The VAB communicates modular craft structure well.** Presets, part count, stage count, craft name, visible assembly, and an editable stage plan make the vehicle differences understandable.
- **Staging has meaningful behavior.** The Strap-On design rewards dropping spent side boosters, later separating the dry core, and hot-staging the Candle upper stage. The tester completed that sequence without ever deploying the parachute.
- **The map view is useful and satisfying.** The successful closed ellipse and atmospheric band are visually readable at a glance.
- **Feedback has personality.** Director Perpetua Naxx's messages are funny without hiding the underlying telemetry; the stable-orbit confirmation feels earned.
- **Fast revert is good.** Esc returns directly to the Assembly Bay with the current craft retained, which makes iterative testing much less painful.

## What could use improvement

### Highest priority

1. **Start mission elapsed time on ignition, not on entering the flight scene.** MET advances while the vehicle sits on the pad. That makes “fastest orbit” depend on UI/OCR/player reaction time and forced multiple restarts during setup. Show separate scene time if desired, but T+ should begin with launch.
2. **Make debug telemetry machine- and human-readable.** The F3 overlay is pale text directly over a changing background and overlaps the ascent panel. The pixel font repeatedly confused `8/a`, `0/O`, signs, and decimals. Give it an opaque high-contrast panel and conventional monospace digits. A local read-only JSON/UDP/named-pipe QA telemetry option would make automated beta testing dramatically more reliable without changing gameplay.
3. **Add a circularization burn estimate.** “Time to apoapsis” is excellent, but a player still has to guess when to start a long, low-thrust burn. Show estimated burn duration and a “start burn in” countdown based on current mass/thrust and the target periapsis.
4. **Distinguish “next stage” from “stage ready.”** The HUD says “drop 2 boosters” from liftoff even while both boosters are producing full thrust. Keep the plan visible, but add a ready/burnout cue or color change so Space is not invited prematurely.

### Balance and usability

5. **Surface pad TWR and expected role next to every preset.** The SRB begins near 3.1 TWR, Heavy around 2.4, and Orbit-Class around 1.09. Orbit-Class therefore feels almost stationary for its first seconds. If that is intentional, label it as an efficiency/advanced vehicle; otherwise its first-stage thrust wants attention.
6. **Clarify preset naming.** The button says “Heavy,” the craft is “Heavy-Lift Orbiter”; “Orbiter” becomes “Orbit-Class Ascent”; “SRB Orbiter” becomes “Strap-On Orbiter.” The designs are good, but consistent button/craft names would reduce selection mistakes.
7. **Improve high-DPI responsiveness.** At Windows display scaling, the right-hand operate/preset panel can clip unless the window is made DPI-aware and moved to a known size. A scale setting or responsive minimum layout would help laptop and accessibility use.
8. **Protect against focus loss during timed runs.** Since MET currently advances continuously, an optional pause-on-focus-loss setting would prevent an alt-tab or tool window from invalidating a speed attempt.

## Tester behavior and usage

The tester is not headless and does not replace the game with its simulation assembly. It uses the actual game window, local `Windows.Media.Ocr`, and physical scan-code input. No model/API/network call occurs in the control loop.

It learned several safeguards during the session:

- validate the selected VAB craft by rendered name before launch;
- prefer high-contrast HUD Ap/Pe/speed over unvalidated F3 readings;
- accept F3 vectors only when they agree with independent HUD magnitudes;
- reject impossible single-frame angle jumps before steering;
- use larger vacuum steering pulses than atmospheric pulses;
- stage by rendered action plus fuel/thrust state, never by a blind timer;
- never press the parachute during ascent;
- cut and revert on stale telemetry, heading divergence, noncompetitive coast, suborbital descent, or best-time expiry.

This is a manually written playtest report; the controller does not generate prose reviews automatically. The controller, immutable benchmark state, curated evidence, and report live in this separate tester repository. No game repository file was edited.
